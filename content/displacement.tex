\section*{Displacement}

\textbf{Displacement} is the change in position of an object, measured as
a straight line from start to finish, \emph{together with its direction}.

\begin{equation}
  \Delta \vec{x} = \vec{x}_{f} - \vec{x}_{i}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Type:} vector (magnitude \emph{and} direction)
  \item \textbf{SI unit:} metre (m)
  \item \textbf{Symbol:} $\Delta x$ or $\vec{s}$
\end{itemize}

Displacement can be positive, negative or zero. It equals the distance
travelled only for straight-line motion without reversing.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Example.} Walk 3 m east, then 4 m west. Distance $= 7\ \text{m}$,
but displacement $= 3 - 4 = -1\ \text{m}$ (1 m west).
\end{quote}

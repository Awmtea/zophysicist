\section*{Acceleration}

\textbf{Acceleration} is the rate at which velocity changes with time.

\begin{equation}
  \vec{a} = \frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{v} - \vec{u}}{t}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Type:} vector
  \item \textbf{SI unit:} $\text{m/s}^{2}$
  \item \textbf{Symbol:} $a$ (or $\vec{a}$)
\end{itemize}

An object accelerates if its speed changes, its direction changes, or
both. Free fall near Earth's surface has
$g \approx 9.8\ \text{m/s}^{2}$ downward.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Example.} A train speeds up from $10$ to $30\ \text{m/s}$ in
$4\ \text{s}$:\quad $a = \dfrac{30-10}{4} = 5\ \text{m/s}^{2}$.
\end{quote}
